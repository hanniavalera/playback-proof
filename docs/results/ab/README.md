# docs/results/ab

The results of the A/B harness ([docs/AB.md](../../AB.md), ADR-140 to ADR-149 in [decisions/ab.md](../../decisions/ab.md)): three A/A pilots of the harness itself (one under high latency, two under the bandwidth drop), two pre-registered experiments, one exploratory run, and the plumbing-only pipeline check that came before them. Every run writes three files here, `<id>.<environment>.<start>.raw.json` (every session, with the plan embedded), `.report.json` and `.report.md`; an A/A run also writes `.calibration.<condition>.md` and `.json`, and a power analysis `.power-<sessions per arm>.md` and `.json`. Any report can be written again from its raw file, byte for byte: `npm run ab -- --report <raw file>`.

**One engine, one machine.** Everything below was measured in Chromium 156.0.8078.4 on `local-darwin-arm64` (macOS 27.2, 12 CPUs, 16 GiB), one browser at a time, on 2026-11-04 (local time is UTC-6). The stream is `vod-cmaf` (a three-rung VP9 ladder of 300, 700 and 1800 kbps, 2 s segments, 60 s), served from localhost. The claims are about **the two configurations as shipped**: configuration B differs from configuration A in four settings at once, and nothing here attributes an effect to one of them. They are not about another engine, another network, or members.

**How to read it.**

- **Startup under high latency (PB-AB-002).** The two configurations start within the 100 ms threshold of each other: the whole 95 % interval of the shift, -19.2 to +51.8 ms, lies inside plus or minus 100 ms, and that is the only verdict that may say there is no meaningful difference. It is not a finding that the shift is zero: the interval says what is ruled out, differences larger than about 52 ms slower or 19 ms faster for B. The arms never started on the same rung (A on 720p in all 30 sessions, B on 240p in all 30), so what is compared is the whole of each configuration, first rung included; the report says so and no rung-matched comparison was possible.
- **Rebuffering under the bandwidth drop (PB-AB-003).** **No session of either configuration stalled** (0 of 39 against 0 of 39 analysed sessions, 40 per arm planned), so the difference is 0 and the interval is the width that 39 sessions without an event allow, -9 to +9 points, inside the 20 point margin. The experiment could only show B stalling more (a share cannot fall below 0), and it did not. Read it as: in this condition, over 40 s of media (about 32 s after the drop), B's share of stalling sessions is not more than about 9 points above A's. Do not read it as: B and A rebuffer equally in general. A alone did not stall in any of the three runs under this condition (see "Surprises").
- **The exploratory run** estimates and decides nothing, and nothing from it is quoted as a finding. On a link of 1500 kbps the first frame of B came 1249 ms earlier than A's (interval -1254 to -1238 ms; the arms start on different rungs, so this includes the first rung), and neither arm stalled (0 of 30 each).
- **The verdict is not the pass mark.** The lab passes a run for being valid. Both experiments were valid, and the A/A checks that they rely on were valid, so each verdict drives the decision of its catalog row: keep A, because nothing worth a change was found.

- **The startup pilot is the check that means something**: the pool varies (start times from 504 to 806 ms), the harness declared 100 of 2000 splits of identical sessions different, 5.00 % at a significance level of 5 %, and the verdicts over the splits were A better 0, B better 0, equivalent 1998, inconclusive 2. A difference of -19.6 ms between two copies of A, with an interval of about 80 ms, is the noise scale of this machine on this metric.
- **The two bandwidth-drop checks pass vacuously, and the calibration does not say so.** No session of configuration A stalled in either pilot (0 of 120), so the share has no variation in the pool and no split can be significant: 0 of 2000 follows from the data, not from the harness being good. What those two runs do show is that the harness does not invent stalls, and that the interleaving, the proofs of the configuration and of the rules, and the plumbing hold for 40 s sessions. It says nothing about the false-positive rate of the test on a share that varies; unit tests, the scipy vectors and the startup calibration cover that. This version of the harness does not write "vacuous" in the calibration itself (ADR-148); it is said here.

## How the claim was made defensible

1. **The order per condition**, readable from `git log`: the plan of an A/A pilot, committed; the pilot; its report committed; the power analysis of its sessions of configuration A committed; only then the plan of the experiment, with its sample size, thresholds, minimum detectable effects and the files they come from, the stopping rule and the A/A report it relies on, committed; only then the experiment; then its data. The commit messages begin "pre-registration before data" or "data". The table below lists every one with its time.
2. **The runner enforces it**: it refuses a plan that git cannot show to be committed and unmodified, the report proves it (gate 1: the plan committed before the run started, at the commit and time shown), and an experiment refuses to start unless the A/A report it names is valid, of this environment and engine, and passed.
3. **No peeking, no re-running to improve a result.** The number of sessions is fixed in the plan; there was no interim analysis, no extension and no re-run of any experiment. The one condition that was piloted twice is the bandwidth drop: its second A/A pilot has a different window and was pre-registered, with its reason, before its data (below); both reports are kept. Nothing invalid was run: every run passed every gate.
4. **A/A first**, with the stop rule that a significant difference between A and A halts the condition. It was not triggered.
5. **Every run is here**, including the pilot whose design turned out to be degenerate, the power analyses at every sample size that was computed, and the experiment during which the machine was overloaded by other processes.
6. **Exploratory is labelled exploratory.** Its plan says in its title that it is exploratory and decides nothing, its report prints no verdict, and nothing from it is quoted as a finding.
7. **B is several settings together** (four, listed in every report): every sentence about B is about the two configurations as shipped. The validator refuses a plan whose text names a single setting, and the report generator is tested so that no setting is named outside the table of the arms and the one sentence that lists all four.
8. **The machine gate was never lowered.** It requires the 1-minute load average before the first session to be at most half the CPUs (6.0 on 12). Every run started below it (the table gives the value, and the `uptime` lines).

### The overload during PB-AB-003 (sessions 72 to 75, about 13:30 to 13:39 local time)

Something other than the harness overloaded the machine for about nine minutes late in PB-AB-003: the 1-minute load average was 195 at 13:39, swap was 11 of 12 GB, and the harness's load reading after the run was 17.0 (the figure in the report). What it did to the run, from the raw results (`PB-AB-003.local-darwin-arm64.20261104T184224Z.raw.json`):

- **Session 73 (arm A) failed**: the player page did not boot (`shaka is not defined`), after 6.3 s. **Session 74 (arm B) failed**: a timeout, after 472.8 s (the plan's limit is 180 s: the whole machine was stalled). Both are in the report as failures, never dropped; the failure guard did not trip (one failure in each arm, 2 of 80).
- **Session 72 (arm A)** finished but took 49.3 s (instead of 40.4), started after 1109 ms (instead of about 210) and left the usual rung path (720p, 360p, 720p, 240p instead of 720p, 360p, 240p). **Session 75 (arm B)** started after 532 ms (instead of about 70) on the usual path.
- **No session of either arm had a rebuffer**, including these four, so the disturbance did not create a stall in the data. Every other session follows the modal path of choices of rung, with the same changes within about a second (below).
- **What it did not change**: the plan fixes the exclusion rule (a failed session is reported and left out of the comparison, nothing else is dropped) and the run passed all seven gates. The analysis therefore stands as pre-registered: 39 sessions per arm analysed. A re-run was not made, because no gate failed and a re-run to be sure of a result is what the discipline forbids; the number that would change it is the number of stalling sessions, and the four disturbed sessions had none. The report's load figures (3.7 before, 17.0 after) show it.

The cause of the peak was not captured. When I looked (13:39) memory was exhausted (swap 11 of 12 GB) and the biggest consumers were the system's indexing, interactive applications and assistant sessions; from 13:47 to 13:52 other agents' gate runs (a `next build` in another repository, a `prettier --check` in the `pb-flake` worktree) held the load near 25. It is recorded, not diagnosed.

## What the pilots changed

- **The exploratory plan needs no minimum detectable effect** (a policy change before any data of Part 2, commit `82c4622`). The schema, as first written, asked an exploratory plan for the minimum detectable effect of a pilot; the third condition was planned as exploratory with no A/A pilot, so the rule was relaxed for a plan that decides nothing. An experiment still needs it. Test and ADR-143.
- **Why there are two pilots under the bandwidth drop.** The first pilot observed 18 s of media, the window of the performance scenario that measures rebuffering on this profile (PB-PERF-003). No session of A stalled (0 of 60), and the rung history shows why that window was too short to see how the player reacts to the drop: the history records when the player changes its choice of rung, and A's first change after the start, to 360p, came between 16.65 and 16.99 s in all 60 sessions, about 8.5 s after the link dropped (at about 8 s) and 1.4 s before the window ended, so whatever the drop does to playback (a second change, a stall) comes after the window closed. The second pilot, with its reason written into its plan before its data, observed 40 s: again no stall (0 of 60), and all 60 sessions changed to 360p (16.7 to 16.9 s) and then to 240p (24.7 to 24.9 s). Both reports are kept. **A correction to that plan's wording:** it says the first window ended "about where the media buffered before the drop was used up"; that was an inference from the 16.9 s timing, and the rung history does not support it (it records the change of choice, not the moment the new rung plays, and the buffer was never measured). What the history supports is the sentence above, and the decision it led to, a longer window, stands. The second pilot sized PB-AB-003 by the rule written in its plan: the smallest even number of sessions per arm, at most 50, whose minimum detectable effect is not above the 20 point threshold: 25.2 points at 30 per arm and 20.1 at 38, 19.0 at 40 (80 % power), so 40 per arm, with a share that can only rise (a share at 0 cannot fall: the power output says so).
- **The pilots' power analyses**: startup under `high-latency` has a minimum detectable effect of 55.7 ms for a rise and 52.8 ms for a fall at 30 per arm, below the 100 ms threshold, so 30 per arm was kept (`PB-AB-001-high-latency.local-darwin-arm64.20261104T171111Z.power-30.json`). The files for the bandwidth drop are `…173140Z.power-30.json` and `.power-38.json` (first pilot) and `…175652Z.power-30.json`, `.power-38.json` and `.power-40.json` (second), the last being the one PB-AB-003 cites. The same structure gave 19.6 points at 38 per arm in one pilot and 20.1 in the other: the minimum has a Monte Carlo error of a few tenths of a point, which is why the plan cites the committed file it was sized on.

## Surprises, and what the experiments cannot say

- **Under the finite bandwidth drop nothing stalled, ever, in either configuration**: 0 stalls in the 120 measured sessions of A in the two pilots, and 0 of 39 in each arm of PB-AB-003. The player's choices of rung (the history records the changes of choice) are reproducible to a fraction of a second. Configuration A chose 720p, then 360p (16.7 to 16.9 s in the 40 s pilot) and 240p (24.7 to 24.9 s); in PB-AB-003, 38 of its 39 analysed sessions followed that path and one (session 72, during the overload) did not. Configuration B chose 240p, then 720p within 0.5 s, 360p between 16.5 and 17.5 s and 240p between 20.6 and 21.6 s, in all 39 analysed sessions: its choice reached the rung the link can carry about four seconds before A's, and neither stalled. A design that cannot show B better (a share at 0 cannot fall) and shows no harm is a legitimate result, not an interesting one.
- **Both arms reacted to the drop at the same moment.** I expected the arms to change rung at different moments after the drop; both changed at about 17 s (A between 16.7 and 16.9 s in the 40 s pilot, B between 16.5 and 17.5 s in PB-AB-003). The reason was not investigated, and no sentence of the plans or reports depends on it. It is why a window of 40 s was enough for both.
- **B starts on a lower rung, and the startup difference is within the margin.** In PB-AB-002 A started on 720p and B on 240p in all 30 sessions of each arm, and the shift is +15 ms (-19.2 to +51.8). Not pre-registered and raw: in PB-AB-003, on the 20 000 kbps link, B's startup was 149 ms shorter than A's (-155 to -146 ms, p below 0.001; the arms start on different rungs, so this includes the first rung); and in PB-AB-002 B made two rung switches more (p below 0.001). They describe these sessions and decide nothing.
- **The A/A calibration can pass vacuously** (above). The check on the share metric under this condition could not have failed.
- **The stall metric in Chromium** counts when the audio runs out, not when the picture freezes (docs/decisions/e2e-rec.md): a video-only underflow is not in the number, so "no stall" is "no stall that the player reported".
- **The exploratory guesses, written before the data, were half right.** The first (the configuration that starts on the higher rung waits longer for its first frame) held: 1698 ms against 450 ms. The second (stalls at the start of the one that starts above what the link carries) did not: no session of either arm stalled (0 of 30 each). In every session both arms changed their first choice within 1.4 s of the start and chose 360p (A: 720p, then 360p; B: 240p, then 360p), a rung the 1500 kbps link carries. The link was constant and the path reproducible; the exploratory run found no difference in rebuffering because there was nothing to find at this link speed.
- **One engine, one machine, one stream, localhost.** WebKit and Firefox were not run. The optional replication on WebKit was left out: the bandwidth-drop design took 2 h 6 min of browser time (the two runs of 19 min 40 s and 43 min 8 s, and 63 min 28 s for the experiment) against the 45 to 55 minutes first estimated, because the window had to outlast the buffer and the sample size came from the pilot; and the machine was overloaded by other processes for part of the afternoon.

## Reproducing

From a clean checkout of this branch, on a quiet machine (1-minute load at most 6.0 on 12 CPUs, so at most half the CPUs), one command at a time, in this order. The plans are in `ab/prereg/` and are committed; the A/A reports and power files they name are in this folder.

```
npm run ab -- --prereg ab/prereg/PB-AB-001-high-latency.json --port 4720
npm run ab -- --prereg ab/prereg/PB-AB-002.json --port 4720
npm run ab -- --prereg ab/prereg/PB-AB-001-bandwidth-drop-finite-40s.json --port 4720
npm run ab -- --prereg ab/prereg/PB-AB-003.json --port 4720
npm run ab -- --prereg ab/prereg/exploratory-constrained-1500.json --port 4720
```

`--out-dir` is `docs/results/ab` by default, so a new run adds files next to these (with a new stamp); it does not overwrite them. The experiments require the A/A report named in `aaCheck.source`, in **this** environment: on another machine or in CI (a different environment id, for instance `gha-linux-x64`) the A/A pilot has to be run there first, its report and power file committed, and a new plan written and committed that names them, as above; running `PB-AB-002.json` there is refused (exit 2) because its A/A check was measured elsewhere. To write a report again from a raw file, with no browser: `npm run ab -- --report docs/results/ab/<id>.<environment>.<stamp>.raw.json`; after the last run, all six reports here were written again this way and compared with the committed ones, byte for byte (the A/A reports read their calibrations from the files next to them), and each gave the exit code of its run (0). To compute a power analysis again: `npm run ab -- --power <raw file> --only-a --sessions-per-arm <n>`.

## `pipeline-check/`: plumbing only, not evidence

[`pipeline-check/`](pipeline-check/pipeline-check.local-darwin-arm64.20261104T164641Z.report.md) is the output of the one real run of Part 1: **a pipeline check**. It exists to show that the harness runs end to end on a real machine, and for nothing else.

- **What it is.** Six sessions of configuration A and B interleaved in one condition (`high-latency`), in Chromium: one warm-up block and two measured blocks, so **two measured sessions per arm**. Plan: [`ab/prereg/pipeline-check.json`](../../../ab/prereg/pipeline-check.json), committed before the run. Command: `npm run ab -- --prereg ab/prereg/pipeline-check.json --port 4720 --out-dir docs/results/ab/pipeline-check`, at commit `1dce76c`, on `local-darwin-arm64` (macOS, 12 CPUs, 16 GiB).
- **Why its numbers mean nothing.** Two sessions per arm cannot say anything about the configurations, and the workstation was shared with two other agents running browsers (1-minute load average 4.0 before the first session and 4.6 after, on 12 CPUs). The report prints its statistics only to show that the report works, prints **no verdict**, and says "pipeline check, plumbing only, not evidence" in its title, its banner, its validity line and the summary the command prints.
- **Nothing was tuned from it.** No threshold, number of sessions, margin or level of the harness was set, changed or checked against these numbers.
- **What it proved.** The service starts as a child process, the player page is built, one Chromium runs the sessions with the arms interleaved, every page reports the configuration of its arm, the service reports the rules it held and the number of requests each fired on, git says the plan was committed before the run, the statistics and the report are produced, and the report is regenerated identically from the raw results. What it found while doing so is in the intro of [decisions/ab.md](../../decisions/ab.md).
